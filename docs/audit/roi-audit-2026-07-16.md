## Findings

*Machine-generated: none (all rule-engine gates passed).*

*Hand-added by audit author from Q4–Q6 output:*

1. **Single-market concentration (Q5).** 97% of resolved UI-matching bets (203 of 210) are Over/Under 2.5 goals. The `1x2` market — nominally covered by the platform — is a loser at −5.00% ROI (UI) and −20.83% ROI (unfiltered, n=14). BetGlitch is empirically an Over/Under 2.5 specialist, not a diversified multi-market predictor. Publishing "AI-powered predictions across markets" overstates coverage.

2. **Filtered-out profitable bucket (Q4).** The 0.55–0.60 confidence bucket has **+40.82% ROI on 57 unfiltered bets** — the strongest bucket in the sample. The current UI threshold `confidence ≥ 0.60` filters this out entirely, mathematically *reducing* the displayed ROI. Contrary to the usual "cherry-picked confidence threshold inflates the number" concern, the current threshold is *too high* relative to observed edge.

3. **Empty high-confidence buckets (Q4, Q8).** The 0.80–1.00 bucket contains 0 bets in both filter variants; the 0.70–0.80 bucket contains only 4. The model rarely produces confidence above 0.70. Suggests either (a) miscalibration — the confidence scale is not fully utilized, or (b) intentional dampening. Worth investigating; if (a), better calibration could produce more high-conviction picks with higher ROI.

4. **Per-league claims are noise-level (Q6).** All 10 top-volume leagues in UI-matching are under the 30-bet threshold. Individual league ROI ranges from −34.82% (Serie B) to +64.10% (La Liga 2). These figures are statistical noise, not signal — no league-specific claim is currently defensible.

5. **Wide confidence interval relative to point estimate (Q2).** UI-matching CI is 2.41% → 30.22% — a 28-point spread. Point estimates like "16%" or "19%" carry a false sense of precision. The honest publishable claim today is "positive edge, likely between 2% and 30%, best estimate ~16%."

6. **Data-quality fix applied mid-audit.** Initial Q5/Q6 output showed win rates of 0.0% everywhere due to a `was_correct` string-coercion bug in the audit script. Fixed in commit `98db895` — now shows realistic rates (58.6% for Over/Under 2.5, 62.1% for Eredivisie, 70.0% for Bundesliga). This does not affect the ROI figures (computed from `profit_loss_10`, which was correctly coerced), but the win-rate column is now trustworthy.

## Recommended actions before publishing (or launching Phase 2)

1. **Reframe messaging** — replace "AI-powered football predictions across all markets" with something honest to the data: "Over/Under 2.5 goals — data-driven picks with an emerging measured edge." Retire multi-market marketing until Q5's numbers spread out.

2. **Investigate lowering the display threshold** from 0.60 to 0.55. Would grow the sample (~210 → ~267), *increase* the measured ROI (0.55–0.60 bucket is +40.82%), *tighten* the CI (bigger n), and remove a threshold that is currently costing the platform its best bucket.

3. **Publish CI, not just point estimate.** Public track record page should read something like: "16% ROI (95% CI: 2%–30%, n=210 resolved bets)." Users' understanding of "our confidence" grows, not shrinks, from showing the bounds.

4. **Remove per-league claims** from the public UI (or add prominent "n<30, not statistically meaningful" flags) until any league reaches n≥30 resolved bets.

5. **Model calibration audit** — investigate why the 0.80–1.00 confidence bucket is empty. Is confidence being clipped upstream? Are ensemble weights damping outputs? A calibrated model should produce SOME high-conviction picks.

6. **Continue collecting data. Re-audit at n≥500 UI-matching bets** (roughly Oct–Nov 2026 at the current ~50 bets/month cadence). At n=500 the CI will tighten to roughly ±5–8 pp, at which point a specific published number becomes defensible.

7. **Do NOT launch Phase 2 (sealed forward-test) until actions 1–3 are implemented.** Sealing a forward-test on top of a misleading display would produce 6 weeks of "verified" data that still overclaims coverage.

## Non-goals (this audit does NOT prove)

- Odds achievability — logged odds are from one bookmaker at fetch time; whether a real user could place a bet at those odds 30 min before kickoff is not tested here.
- Real-world friction — no accounting for bookmaker limits/bans, minimum stakes, currency conversion, tax.
- Prospective validity — everything is retrospective. Only a sealed forward-test (Phase 2) can answer whether the future looks like the past.
- Model attribution — the audit measures pipeline output, not which model/signal is producing the edge.
